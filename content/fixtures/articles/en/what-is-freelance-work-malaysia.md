# What counts as freelance work in Malaysia?

"Freelance", "gig", "contractor" and "part-time" get used as if they mean the same thing. They do not, and the difference decides who handles tax, EPF and SOCSO, who carries the risk if a job falls through, and whether a client can tell you when to work.

## What makes work "freelance"?

Freelance work is work you take on as an independent party: you agree the scope, you decide how and when it gets done, you supply your own tools, and you can usually take other clients. The client buys a result, not your day.

The practical test is control. If the client sets your hours, your location, your leave and your day-to-day instructions, the arrangement looks more like employment whatever the invoice says. Employment also brings statutory contributions and protections; freelance work generally does not.

## How is that different from a gig or a contract for service?

- A **gig** is short and platform- or app-mediated: a delivery, a shift, a single shoot. It can still be freelance work.
- A **contract for service** is the legal description of freelance work: a defined deliverable for an agreed fee.
- A **contract of service** is employment: ongoing, controlled, with statutory contributions.

Only the last one is employment. The first two are arrangements where you are responsible for your own tax filing and your own safety net.

## Why does the label matter?

Three things change at once:

1. **Tax.** As an independent earner you declare business or freelance income and keep records of expenses that are wholly and necessarily incurred. An employer, by contrast, deducts monthly tax through PCB.
2. **Contributions.** Employment usually means EPF and SOCSO contributions. Freelancers generally arrange their own savings and insurance unless they register voluntarily.
3. **Risk.** A client can end a freelance engagement at the scope boundary. An employee has notice and termination rules.

None of this is advice for your specific situation. If the amount is significant or the arrangement is murky, ask LHDN or a licensed adviser rather than a forum thread.

## How do you work out which one a job is?

Use this order before you quote:

1. Read the brief for control words: fixed hours, mandatory office days, "report to", rota, leave approval.
2. Check what you are paid for: a deliverable and a fee, or time and attendance.
3. Check who supplies tools and materials.
4. Check whether you may work for other clients.
5. Write the answers into the scope sheet and confirm them in writing before work starts.

If the answers point to employment but the client wants an invoice, that is a conversation to have before the first invoice, not after.

## What it takes

Classifying the arrangement takes one conversation and a short written scope. Getting it wrong is the expensive part: unpaid contributions, a misfiled tax year, or a dispute with no paper trail. Keep the brief, the agreed fee, the deliverable list and every change request in one place.

## FAQ

### Is freelance work legal in Malaysia?

Yes. Independent work is a normal way to earn; what differs is the paperwork — you register a business name if you trade under one, declare the income, and arrange your own contributions rather than having an employer do it.

### Do freelancers need to register with SSM?

If you carry on business under a trade name, registration is generally required. Working under your own full name as an individual is treated differently. Check the current SSM guidance for your case; the rules and thresholds are theirs to state.

### Can a client treat a freelancer like an employee?

A contract can say anything, but the working arrangement is what usually decides the classification. Fixed hours, direct control and integration into the team are the patterns that cause problems. If a role looks like employment, ask for an employment contract instead of an invoice arrangement.

### Do freelancers pay EPF and SOCSO?

Not automatically through a client the way an employee does. There are voluntary schemes, and self-employed contributors can arrange their own. Confirm the current options with EPF and SOCSO before relying on them.
