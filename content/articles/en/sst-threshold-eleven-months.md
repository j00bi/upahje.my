# SST counts the last eleven months, not the tax year

Service tax is **8%**, or **6%** for food and beverage, logistics, telecommunications and parking, and **RM25 per credit or charge card**. The registration threshold is not measured on your financial year. It is measured on a window of eleven months plus this one, or this one plus the eleven after it.

That single mechanic decides whether a growing shop has a compliance date this year or next, and it is the part most owners get wrong: they check the year-end accounts and conclude they are under the line, when the line is tested monthly against a moving twelve-month total.

## The two methods, as RMCD states them

The Customs FAQ sets out both, and they are not interchangeable:

**Historical method** — the value of taxable services for any month, plus the value for the eleven months before it. Their example: July 2024 combined with August 2023 through June 2024.

**Future method** — the value for any month, plus the eleven months after it. Their example: July 2024 through June 2025.

The historical method is the one that matters for a shop already trading: it tells you, month by month, whether your trailing twelve months has crossed the threshold. The future method exists for cases where the forward book is known — a contract signed, a pipeline committed.

## What the official pages state, and what they leave out

Read on 27 November 2026, these are the figures the Customs pages actually print:

| What | Figure | Where it applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Service tax rate | 8% on taxable services generally | Service tax |
| Reduced rate | 6% for F&B, logistics, telecommunications, parking | Service tax |
| Credit and charge cards | RM25 per card | Service tax |
| Cafeteria food services | Taxed when sales value exceeds RM1,500,000 | Service tax |
| Sales tax registration | When taxable goods sales exceed RM500,000 over 12 months | Sales tax |

What they do **not** print, on the pages we read, is the general service-tax registration threshold — the number that applies to a consultant, a contractor, a repair shop or a software business. Several secondary sources say RM500,000; that is the sales-tax figure appearing in the same breath, and we are not going to carry it across a category boundary on somebody's blog.

So this article states the mechanic, which is verified, and refuses the number, which is not. If you need the threshold for your specific service category today, the answer is in the industry guide for that category, and it has to be read, not assumed.

## Why the window matters more than the number

A rolling test changes the decision in a way an annual test does not.

A shop doing RM40,000 a month in taxable services is at RM480,000 on a trailing twelve months — under a RM500,000 line, and comfortably so, until the month it is not. Add one good month at RM70,000 and the trailing total becomes RM510,000. The obligation attaches from the crossing, not from the next financial year, and the invoice you issue in that month is the one that should have carried tax.

That is the failure mode: not a shop that ignored the rules, but a shop that checked once, in the wrong month, against the wrong window.

## Run the eleven-month total before you register anything

1. **Total your taxable services month by month**, for the last twelve months. If your accounting software reports quarterly, get the monthly numbers out of it — the quarterly figure cannot answer a rolling question.
2. **Add the current month to the eleven before it.** That is the historical method, and it is the number that decides registration.
3. **Watch the trend, not just the total.** Three months of growth at 10% moves a shop from safe to in-scope inside one window.
4. **If you cross it, register through MySST**, and treat the crossing month's invoices as the ones to review first.

e-Invoice is a separate obligation with a separate trigger, and the [RM3 million turnover exemption](https://upahje.my/en/articles/e-invoice-exemption-rm3-million) has nothing to do with this test. A shop can be under the e-Invoice exemption and over the service-tax threshold at the same time, which is exactly the kind of overlap that gets missed when the two are treated as one compliance calendar.
