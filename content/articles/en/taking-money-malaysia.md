# Taking money: FPX costs a ringgit, cards cost 2%

Two payment methods, two shapes of cost. An FPX payment is a flat **RM1.00** at CHIP or **RM0.75** to **RM1.25** at Billplz depending on the plan, and it lands in your account the next business day. A card payment is a percentage — **2.0%** at CHIP, **3% plus RM1.00** at Stripe — and it settles in **two business days**. The fee is the visible difference. The two-day gap is the one that hurts a shop with thin cash.

## The fee shape decides the basket size it suits

A flat fee hurts small baskets and is irrelevant on a large one. A percentage is the reverse.

On a RM30 basket, CHIP's FPX fee is RM1.00, or 3.3% of the bill. Its credit-card fee is RM0.60, or 2.0%. The card is cheaper per transaction at RM30 — and then CHIP's own note applies: "Transaction fees are subject to 8% SST", which takes the card fee to about RM0.65.

On a RM150 basket the relationship flips hard: FPX stays RM1.00, while the card fee is RM3.00, or RM3.24 with SST. The percentage does not care that the basket grew; it grows with it.

## Settlement is a property of the method, not the gateway

This is where a single "X days" figure per gateway would be wrong, because the same provider prints different terms for different methods.

| Gateway | Method | Fee as printed | Settlement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CHIP | FPX B2C | RM1.00 | every next day |
| CHIP | Local credit card | 2.0% | every 2 business days |
| CHIP | Local debit card | 1.0% | every 2 business days |
| CHIP | DuitNow QR | 1.0% | every next day |
| Billplz | FPX B2C | RM1.25 Basic, RM0.75 Standard | next business day |
| Billplz | Card, MYR | 1.8% Basic, 1.5% Standard | T+2 business days |
| Stripe | Domestic cards | 3% + RM1.00 | rolling, weekly or monthly |
| senangPay | FPX and cards | platform fee from RM199/year; MDR not published | next working day stated |

Stripe's payout schedule is a setting, not a fixed term, so any article that prints "Stripe settles in two days" is wrong in a way a reader can disprove from their own dashboard.

## Three gateways will not tell you their price

Of the six gateways read for this piece, three publish a rate you can plan from: CHIP, Billplz and Stripe. senangPay publishes a platform fee of RM199 a year and not the per-transaction rate; iPay88's pricing page returned 404 and no public fee schedule was found; toyyibPay's pages served JavaScript shells with no fee text.

Those three are quote-based. A shop comparing six gateways on price is comparing three published numbers and three sales conversations, and the piece does not rank the conversations.

## What the two-day gap costs

A shop taking RM1,000 a day on cards has RM2,000 in transit on any given day, and that money is not in the account when the supplier invoice arrives on the second day. On FPX the same shop has one day of transit instead.

That is the cash-flow bite: not the 2% fee, which the customer effectively pays in the basket, but the two days of float the card network keeps.

## What to do before you pick one

1. Read the settlement line for each method, not the gateway headline.
2. Ask whether the printed fee includes SST, and get the answer in writing.
3. Work out both fee shapes on your two most common basket sizes.
4. Count how many days of card revenue you can float without borrowing.
5. Default to FPX where the customer's bank is on it, and treat cards as the convenience option with a cost.

For a small shop, FPX is the default and cards are a service you offer at a price. The fee difference is visible and easy to argue about; the settlement difference is quiet, and it is the one that decides whether you can pay the next supplier on time.
