# What automation work costs in Malaysia: published rates, six sources

Published prices for automation work in Malaysia run from RM400 for a simple process build to RM6,000-plus for autonomous task execution. Those figures and 28 more below come from six Malaysian publishers, with the publisher, unit and date on every row. Four are studios, one is an independent practitioner, and one is a freelance profile; the only per-hour figure comes from that profile. Solo freelancers usually quote per job rather than publish, so this table is published supply, not the whole market.

## Who publishes these numbers, and who does not?

The figures below come from pricing pages of Malaysian automation providers and from one Malaysian freelance profile. Every row carries the date it was read: 19 November 2026 for the studio and platform pages, and 27 October 2026 for the independent practitioner's page.

The global marketplaces are unmeasured here. Upwork returns HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare block, Fiverr returns a bot challenge, Jobstreet returns a Cloudflare interstitial, and Indeed and Maukerja render their listings only in JavaScript. Not one Upwork or Fiverr profile rate could be collected, so nothing here should be read as what a globally-priced freelancer charges. These bands describe the local published-supply layer: agencies with a price list, and one marketplace profile with an hourly rate.

Two further exclusions. Every "quote on request" tier is excluded, because a quote is not a rate. Freelancer.com profiles that price in USD are excluded, even where the person is Malaysian, because the number is not a Malaysian price.

## What does an integration cost?

## Why do the same tasks carry different units?

Because the publishers are selling different things: a session of someone's time, a project with a defined end, or an ongoing subscription.

RM400 per 4-hour session and RM1,500 per project are not competing prices. The first is labour, and you can buy two sessions and still have no working system. The second assumes a defined scope. The third, a monthly retainer, buys attention rather than delivery. Comparing them without converting to what you get is how buyers misread a table like this.

The dates matter for the same reason. Several pages carry no publication date, so the row date is the day the price was read, not the day it was published. A price read on 19 November 2026 may have been written in 2025 and not updated.

## What changes the price of a quote?

Six things, and each one appears in the table above as a different tier rather than a discount.

1. How many systems are connected. Every publisher prices by process or workflow count, not by hours.
2. Whether approvals are involved. KodeSME's growth tier adds approval flows and a dashboard at a higher price than the starter tier.
3. Whether a human reviews the output. Document extraction with a review step is priced per thousand pages by BlueAura.
4. Whether the work is monitored after launch. Every publisher here has a monthly tier; some call it a care plan, some a retainer.
5. Whether the build touches existing accounting or project software. That is the top of Pang Jun Han's range, RM1,600 to RM4,000.
6. Whether you buy a session or a scope. The only per-hour figure in the set belongs to a freelance profile, not to any of the studios.

## How do I compare two quotes when the units differ?

Convert both to the same three numbers before you compare: total cost to a working system, monthly cost after launch, and what each one excludes.

A RM400 session and a RM1,500 project both look small next to a RM6,000 build, and none of them is comparable until you ask how many sessions until it runs, how many months of retainer you will pay, and which of the six factors above each quote excludes. Write the answers next to the figures from the same date, and the comparison stops being about which number is lower.

## FAQ

### Is RM60 an hour typical for Malaysian automation freelancers?

It is one published figure from one profile on freelancing.my, checked 19 November 2026, and it is the only hourly rate in this set of six publishers. One row is not a typical rate. Treat it as an anchor for what one practitioner asks, not as a market.

### Why are there no Upwork or Fiverr figures?

Because they could not be read from here, and the article says so in the method section rather than filling the gap with a number from memory.

### Does a higher monthly retainer mean a better system?

No, and this table cannot tell you that. It lists prices, publishers and dates. It says nothing about the quality of the work, delivery time, or whether the automation still runs a year later, and none of the six publishers publishes that information.
