# Grab and foodpanda commission rates (2026)

Neither Grab nor foodpanda prints a merchant commission on any page a business can read. The only citable commission figures in Malaysia come from the competition regulator's own foodpanda decision, in bands from 1% to 25%, and at the top of that band a RM25 order leaves RM8.50 before labour, rent or electricity.

## The platforms publish nothing

Grab's Malaysian merchant page sells the programme and prints no rate: the word commission does not appear in the served text, and there is no rate card on the page. foodpanda's merchant page returned HTTP 403 to this client, so its rate is not readable at all.

That matters because the common sentence in Malaysian guides, that GrabFood takes a certain percentage, is never published. It comes from a merchant's contract, a vendor's estimate, or folklore, and none of those can be checked by a reader deciding whether to sign.

## The regulator's numbers are bands, and the names are redacted

The MyCC decision on foodpanda records five anonymised franchised merchants with commission bands: 1% to 5%, 10% to 25%, 15% to 20%, and twice 15% to 25%. The merchant names are redacted, which tells you the actual rates are contractual and confidential.

The decision also records the finding that foodpanda "imposes higher commission rates on independent restaurants than on franchised restaurants due to a disparity in bargaining power". The Commission found no infringement, so this is a description of the market, not a penalty.

The decision itself is dated 11 September 2023; the case list carries 13 September 2023, which is the date the press reported it.

## What the top of the band costs on a real basket

The arithmetic below applies the 25% upper edge and a 20% midpoint, with packaging at RM1.50 per order and food cost at 35% of menu price. Packaging and food cost are assumptions, not published figures; commission is the regulator's band.

| Basket | Commission at 25% | Commission at 20% | Packaging | Net after commission and packaging, at 25% / 20% | Food cost at 35% | Left before labour and rent, at 25% / 20% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RM15 | RM3.75 | RM3.00 | RM1.50 | RM9.75 / RM10.50 | RM5.25 | RM4.50 / RM5.25 |
| RM25 | RM6.25 | RM5.00 | RM1.50 | RM17.25 / RM18.50 | RM8.75 | RM8.50 / RM9.75 |
| RM45 | RM11.25 | RM9.00 | RM1.50 | RM32.25 / RM34.50 | RM15.75 | RM16.50 / RM18.75 |

Each row subtracts commission, then packaging, then food cost, in that order, so a reader can redo any cell with a calculator. Read the last column as what is left to pay for the cook, the rent and the gas. On a RM15 order at the top of the band it is RM4.50, which is not a margin; it is a warning that small baskets carry fixed costs badly.

The 35% food cost is the weakest number here, and it is ours. A business with a different food cost should redo the row with its own figure, which is why it is in a column of its own rather than folded into a total.

## What is not published

Promo co-funding: neither platform states whether the merchant funds part of a platform promotion, or at what share, and the MyCC decision references a promotional-expense spreadsheet whose values are redacted. No percentage is printed here.

Delivery fee: neither platform page states who sets it or who keeps it, so no claim is made about it.

GrabFood's rate: no MyCC decision and no platform page, so there is nothing citable at all.

## What to ask before signing

1. The commission rate in writing, per category, not a range.
2. Whether the rate changes with exclusivity, and what the non-exclusive rate is.
3. Who pays for promotions, and at what share.
4. Who sets the delivery fee and who keeps it.
5. The rate for a small basket, in ringgit, at your menu prices.

A platform that will not put the rate beside a worked basket is telling you something. The honest answer to "what will I keep on a RM25 order" is a number, and a sales page that will not produce it is asking you to sign a contract you cannot price.
