# SOCSO stops at RM6,000, and nobody tells the new hire

An employer pays 1.75% of monthly wages into SOCSO and deducts 0.5% from the employee, up to a wage ceiling of RM6,000 that took effect on 1 October 2024. EIS is 0.2% each side, on the same ceiling. Above RM6,000 every one of those lines is flat, so a raise costs the employer nothing extra in SOCSO or EIS.

## The rate follows the wage until the ceiling

Below RM6,000 the contribution is a percentage of the wage, and at RM6,000 it stops moving. The table applies the published rates to four wages; the schedule amounts differ by a few sen because SOCSO is table-based, not flat arithmetic.

| Monthly wage | Employer SOCSO at 1.75% | Employee SOCSO at 0.5% | EIS each side at 0.2% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RM3,000 | RM52.50 | RM15.00 | RM6.00 |
| RM5,999 | RM104.98 | RM30.00 | RM12.00 |
| RM6,000 | RM105.00 | RM30.00 | RM12.00 |
| RM8,000 | RM105.00 | RM30.00 | RM12.00 |

The last two rows are the point. A RM2,000 raise costs the employer nothing in SOCSO and nothing in EIS, which is why the cap matters more to a senior hire than to a junior one.

## Two categories, and the one that covers injury only

First Category, for employees under 60, covers both the employment injury scheme and the invalidity scheme at 1.75% employer and 0.5% employee.

Second Category, for employees aged 60 and over, is the injury scheme only: 1.25% payable by the employer, with no employee share.

Putting a 60-year-old on the First Category row overpays; putting a 25-year-old on the Second Category row leaves them uncovered for invalidity.

## EIS is separate, and smaller

EIS is 0.4% in total, split 0.2% employer and 0.2% employee, and it shares the RM6,000 ceiling. It is not SOCSO and it does not appear in a SOCSO table, which is why payroll summaries that show one line for "SOCSO/EIS" hide which scheme a ringgit went to.

## What the cap is worth at two wages

At RM4,500 the employer pays RM78.75 in SOCSO. At RM7,500 the employer pays RM105.00, which is 1.4%, and the employee pays RM30.00, which is 0.4% rather than the headline 0.5%.

The percentage printed on a rate card is a rate up to the ceiling, not a rate on every salary. A quotation that multiplies 1.75% by a RM9,000 salary overstates the SOCSO line by RM52.50 a month, and the same mistake on EIS overstates it by RM6.00.

## What is not confirmed here

Registration timing is marked [VERIFY]: PERKESO's employer-registration page was not read for this article, so no deadline is printed. Employers still have to register, and the deadline should be checked before the first payroll month rather than after it.

Schedule amounts are also not reproduced: the table applies the percentages, and PERKESO's schedule is authoritative for the ringgit figures.

## What to do before the first payslip

1. Put each employee in the right category before anything else.
2. Apply the percentage up to RM6,000 and stop there.
3. Keep EIS on its own line, because it is a different scheme.
4. Check the schedule for the exact amount, and expect a few sen difference.
5. Confirm the registration deadline at PERKESO directly before the first payroll.

My opinion, stated once: the ceiling is the only part of this that changes how you hire. Below RM6,000 the contribution tracks the salary, but above it the scheme is a fixed cost, so the marginal cost of a senior hire is lower than a payroll spreadsheet suggests, and the marginal cost of a junior one is higher.
