# EPF contribution: 12% or 13%, and RM5,000 is the line

An employer pays 13% of monthly wages when the wage is RM5,000 or less, and 12% when it is more. The employee pays 11% at both levels. The ringgit amount on the payslip is not that multiplication, though: it comes from the Third Schedule and is rounded up to the next ringgit.

## The split happens at RM5,000 exactly

RM5,000 is inside the 13% band, because the rule reads "RM5,000 and below". One ringgit above it moves the employer to 12%, which is why a small raise can cost the employer less than a small raise at the boundary suggests.

The table applies the published percentages to five wages so the break is visible. The last column matters more than the others: the schedule amount is what gets paid.

| Monthly wage | Employer rate | Employer amount at the rate | Employee amount at 11% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RM2,000 | 13% | RM260.00 | RM220.00 |
| RM4,999 | 13% | RM649.87 | RM549.89 |
| RM5,000 | 13% | RM650.00 | RM550.00 |
| RM5,001 | 12% | RM600.12 | RM550.11 |
| RM6,000 | 12% | RM720.00 | RM660.00 |

The employee's 11% does not move at RM5,000; only the employer's rate does, so a wage just above the line does not reduce take-home pay.

Those amounts are arithmetic at the published rate, not the schedule. KWSP computes contributions from the Third Schedule for wages up to RM20,000, and the schedule total is rounded up to the next ringgit, so expect the payslip to differ by a few sen.

## Two bands that change the answer

Employees aged 60 and over sit in Part E of the schedule: the employer pays 4% and the employee pays nothing. Non-Malaysians registered from 1 August 1998 sit in Part F, at 2% employer and 2% employee. Both are schedule amounts, not multiplications.

Neither band appears in a payroll template built for a young all-Malaysian team, which is how a first payslip for a returning retiree goes wrong.

## The deadline is the 15th

Contributions are due on or before the 15th of the following month. KWSP's own example is January wages, February contribution month, paid by 15 February.

Late payment is a different charge from the contribution itself, and this article does not estimate it. Keep proof of payment every month, because disputes usually arrive months later.

## What the boundary costs in a month

At RM5,000 the employer pays RM650.00 at the rate. At RM5,001 the employer pays RM600.12, because the whole wage moves to the 12% band rather than the excess above RM5,000.

That is a RM49.88 drop for a RM1 raise, and it is the one place where paying someone more can cost the business less in EPF. The schedule smooths some of the gap, but the band change is real.

Over a year at a constant wage the gap is RM598.56, and that figure is arithmetic on the published percentages rather than a schedule amount. The Third Schedule is what actually gets paid, and it rounds up to the next ringgit, so the payslip lands within a few sen of these numbers instead of exactly on them.

One more edge: above RM20,000 a month, KWSP stops using the schedule and the exact percentage applies, which is the only band where the multiplication on the page is the amount paid. A payroll template that only knows the schedule will need a second rule for the highest salaries.

## The 60-plus line, worked once

For an employee aged 60 or over at RM3,000, Part E puts the employer rate at 4%; the schedule entry for that wage is RM120, and the employee contributes nothing. The same wage under the 13/11 pair costs the employer RM390 and the employee RM330.

The difference is not a rounding error; it is a band, and it is decided by the employee's age on the contribution month, not by the payroll template's default. A template built for a young team will miss this line until someone checks it by hand.

## What to do before the first payslip

1. Find the wage in the Third Schedule, not on a calculator.
2. Round up to the next ringgit as the schedule does.
3. Pay by the 15th of the following month.
4. Check whether anyone on the payroll is 60 or over, or a non-Malaysian, before using the 13/11 pair.
5. Keep the schedule version you used, because a rate change does not rewrite history.

My opinion, stated once: the 12-versus-13 question is the easy half. The half that costs money is the schedule and the two exceptions, because those are the lines a template gets wrong quietly, and a payroll error is discovered by an employee, not by an auditor.
