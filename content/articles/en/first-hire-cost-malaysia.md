# A first hire at RM3,000 costs RM3,448.50 a month

A RM3,000 salary costs the business RM3,448.50 a month before any allowance, and the person receives RM2,626.50. The gap is four statutory lines, and one of them is a deduction most payroll templates do not carry: LINDUNG 24 Jam, a cover paid entirely by the employee.

## The employer pays 14.95% above the salary

The employer's share is EPF at 13%, SOCSO at 1.75% and EIS at 0.2% of the wage, because a RM3,000 monthly wage sits below the RM5,000 line where EPF drops to 12%. Those three lines add RM448.50 to the RM3,000.

The employee's side is larger than it looks. EPF takes 11%, SOCSO 0.5%, EIS 0.2%, and LINDUNG 24 Jam another 0.75% in its first phase. That is RM373.50, so the take-home is RM2,626.50.

## Two of the four lines are capped

SOCSO and EIS are capped at an assumed monthly wage of RM6,000, a ceiling that took effect on 1 October 2024. Above that wage the contributions stay flat in ringgit, which makes them proportionally cheaper for a senior hire and proportionally painful for a first hire near the floor.

SOCSO also has a second category: an employer contribution of 1.25% that covers the injury scheme only, for employees aged 60 and over.

## The payslip line most templates miss

LINDUNG 24 Jam covers accidents outside work and not related to the job, around the clock, for eligible employees. The employee pays the whole contribution, which is why it never appears in an employer-cost calculator.

It is phased: 0.75% for the first two years, 1.0% for the next three, and 1.25% from the sixth year. Employees already on the payroll stayed in the scheme unless they opted out during the window that ran from 13 July to 31 August 2026.

A first hire joining today does not get that window. PERKESO's own page states that local employees employed for the first time after 31 August 2026 are required to contribute under SKBBK, so the 0.75% belongs on the payslip from the first month.

## What the RM3,000 hire looks like

| Line | Employer pays | Employee pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross salary | RM3,000.00 | — |
| EPF 13% / 11% | RM390.00 | RM330.00 |
| SOCSO 1.75% / 0.5% | RM52.50 | RM15.00 |
| EIS 0.2% / 0.2% | RM6.00 | RM6.00 |
| LINDUNG 24 Jam 0.75% | — | RM22.50 |
| Total | RM3,448.50 | RM373.50 |

The percentages are as published, but the ringgit amounts on a real payslip come from the contribution schedule, which is table-based and rounds up to the next ringgit. Expect the schedule to differ from flat arithmetic by a few sen, not by ringgit.

At RM2,000 the same arithmetic gives an employer cost of RM2,299.00 and an employee deduction of RM249.00. The employer share stays at 14.95%, because every line below the ceiling is a flat percentage of the wage.

## Two lines with no number here

The HRD Corp levy starts at five Malaysian employees: optional at five to nine at 0.5%, compulsory at ten at 1%, with Form 1 reviewed within 30 days. A first hire sits below both bands, which is why no levy appears in the totals above.

PCB, the monthly tax deduction, is computed from LHDN's MTD schedule. The page I tried returned 404, so the take-home figures above are before tax, and this article does not estimate a tax number.

## What to do before the first payslip

1. Register the employer with EPF and PERKESO before the first salary month, not after it.
2. Pay the contribution on or before the 15th of the following month; the KWSP page's own example is January salary, February contribution, paid by 15 February.
3. Add LINDUNG 24 Jam to the payslip template at 0.75%, and put the phase change in a calendar note for two years out.
4. Use the schedule amount for SOCSO and EIS, not a flat percentage, and round up as the schedule does.
5. Budget the employer cost, not the salary, when you decide what you can afford to hire.

My opinion, stated once: the salary is the smaller number in this decision. A business that budgets RM3,000 and discovers RM3,448.50 after the offer has already spent the difference on something else, and the first place it cuts is the training the new hire needed.
