# Five side-income skills in Malaysia that actually pay in 2026

Five skills clear a two-listing bar on the one Malaysian freelance platform whose public pages could be read: video editing, graphic design, data entry, cold calling and app testing. Every band below is a count, not an average. The evidence is 12 listings on freelancing.my, posted between September and November 2026, each with its own budget in ringgit.

## Who these numbers come from?

One platform, freelancing.my, and every figure is a budget printed on a public project listing rather than a survey answer.

That is a narrower base than it sounds, and the gap matters. The global marketplaces could not be read: Upwork returns HTTP 403 with a Cloudflare block, Fiverr returns a bot challenge, Jobstreet returns a Cloudflare interstitial, and Indeed and Maukerja render their job cards only in JavaScript. Delivery platforms such as Grab and Lalamove publish per-trip fares only inside their driver apps. So this article describes what Malaysian clients publicly budget on one local platform, not what all Malaysian side income pays.

Three exclusions, all deliberate. Listings advertising RM0.00 or RM1.00 are placeholders and were dropped. Listings located outside Malaysia were dropped. Commission-only posts with no stated base rate were dropped, because "salary plus commission" is not a number a reader can plan around.

## Which five skills clear the bar?

A skill qualified only if at least two listings with a real ringgit budget were posted for it. Five did.

## What does video editing pay?

Three listings, RM150 to RM2,500 per project, and the spread is scope rather than skill: a short-form trial batch, a full short film, and a shoot-and-edit role.

The lowest is RM150 for a short-form TikTok or Reels trial batch, which is a paid test before ongoing work. The middle is RM1,500 for editing a 30 to 40 minute romance-comedy. The highest is RM2,500 for a videographer, editor and on-camera talent role, which is three jobs in one listing.

Getting employable: our estimate is two to four months of deliberate practice on one editing tool, longer if you have never cut to a brief. The listings ask for samples, not certificates, so a portfolio of three finished pieces matters more than a course.

## What does graphic design pay?

Two listings, RM300 and RM1,100 per project, and the difference is volume: a set of retail point-of-sale items against a monthly content batch.

RM300 covers a booth, tent cards, banners and an illustration for a retail promotion. RM1,100 covers outdoor adventure content produced as a monthly batch, which is recurring work rather than a one-off.

Getting employable: our estimate is one to three months if you already draw or use design software, and the barrier is canva-level output versus print-ready files. Retail work asks for the second.

## What does data entry pay?

Two listings, RM50 and RM70 per project, and both are small one-off tasks rather than an income you can build on.

RM50 is a simple verification task. RM70 is for local coordinators on a purchase project across four countries. Neither states hours, so neither can be converted to an hourly rate honestly.

Getting employable: today. The barrier is not skill, it is that the work is intermittent, low-paid and competes with anyone with a browser. Treat it as a first step to a better-paying listing, not as a side income in itself.

## What does cold calling pay?

Two listings, RM700 and RM1,000 per project, both advertised budgets rather than agreed fees, and both likely to be part-time hours.

RM700 is a part-time remote sales development role advertised as no experience needed. RM1,000 is a caller for a web development and SEO agency. The listings give no hours, so the hourly value is unknown.

Getting employable: today, with a script and a clear voice. The risk is that commission structures replace the budget after the first month, which is why the two rows are marked as advertised rather than agreed.

## What does app testing pay?

Two listings, RM330 and RM600 per project, and both are Malaysian-specific work: language and locale testing that a global tester cannot do.

RM330 is UX research on trading platforms for Malaysian testers only. RM600 is Melayu-language testing of an AI application. The premium over generic testing comes from being the right kind of Malaysian, not from testing skill.

Getting employable: days to weeks. What the listings ask for is device access and language, not a testing qualification.

## What does none of this tell you?

It tells you what clients publicly budget on one platform in one quarter, and nothing about what they pay the person they actually hire, how many hours the work takes, or how often a listing turns into ongoing work.

Two further limits. The two cold-calling rows are advertised budgets, which a client can lower once they see the response. And no listing states hours, so any hourly figure here would be arithmetic on a guess. If you need an hourly number, ask the client before quoting, because the listing will not give it to you.

## FAQ

### Is one platform enough to judge the whole market?

No, and this article does not claim it is. It is the layer that could be read on 19 November 2026. A reader who finds better-paying listings elsewhere has found exactly the gap this piece names, and the honest response is to check those listings yourself rather than trust a single table.

### Why are some rates marked as advertised budgets?

Because a listing's stated budget is what the client says they will pay, not what was agreed. Two cold-calling rows fall in that category, and they are labelled so a reader does not treat a wish as a rate.

### Can I combine several of these skills?

Yes, and the listings suggest it is common: the RM2,500 video role combines filming, editing and appearing on camera. A second skill raises the price faster than doing the first one for longer.
