# Billed by contacts, sold as an operating system

KuasaOS launched in October 2026 as "Malaysia's First AI Agents-Powered Business OS", with three agents given names — ARA, HIRA and SAFA — and a launch offer of fifty million free AI credits to the first thousand businesses. It is a real product from a real company, FunnelEvo Sdn Bhd, and it ships weekly.

Our view, stated once: it is a CRM with a costume on.

The evidence is not a leak or an insider. It is on the vendor's own pages, read on 26 November 2026.

## The meter tells you what a product is

Look at how something is priced and you learn what it thinks it is selling. Kuasa's plans:

| Plan | Price | Contacts | Notable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Free | RM0 | 250 | lifetime trial |
| Starter | RM49/month, billed yearly | 1,500 | automations, WhatsApp API, remove branding |
| Professional | RM199/month, billed yearly | 4,500 | 3 chatbots, 15 order forms, AI landing pages |
| Business | RM499/month, billed yearly | 10,000 | 5 chatbots, 35 order forms |

Every tier is metered by **contact count**. Not by agents run, not by documents processed, not by invoices issued, not by payslips calculated. Contacts. That is the meter a contact database uses, because contacts are the thing a contact database has.

An operating system would meter work: payroll runs, invoices filed, returns submitted, reconciliations closed. This one meters a list.

## The product's own words disagree with the launch's words

The pricing page footer, written by the company, reads:

> "The easy and effective CRM for closing deals"

The launch page, written by the same company in the same quarter, reads:

> "Say hello to your AI-powered business operating system."

One of those sentences is a description and the other is a category claim. A CRM for closing deals is a perfectly good product. Calling it an operating system is a positioning decision, and positioning is free — which is why it is the first thing every launch changes.

## What the feature list actually contains

We read the full comparison table. Counting mentions of the things a Malaysian business must do to stay out of trouble:

| Term | Mentions on the pricing page |
| --- | --- |
| payroll | 0 |
| PCB | 0 |
| EPF | 0 |
| SOCSO | 0 |
| SST | 0 |
| e-Invoice | 0 |
| accounting | 0 |
| HR | 0 |
| employee | 0 |

What is there: contacts, AI scoring, chatbots, order forms, AI landing pages, team members, custom fields, broadcasts, automations, WhatsApp API access.

None of that is worthless. All of it is a sales and marketing stack. A business in Malaysia does not get through a month on a sales stack, because the month also contains a payroll run, an SST return if it is registered, and — since 2024 — e-Invoice obligations that arrive whether or not your lead pipeline is full.

## The launch was an offer, not a capability

The October launch led with **fifty million AI credits for the first thousand businesses**, **up to 50% off**, and a hero section carrying four counters: total registrations, records under management, countries, years in business. On 26 November 2026, all four read **0**. That may be a rendering fault rather than a fact, but it is the version of the page the vendor is serving.

"Malaysia's first" is not a claim that can be checked, only asserted, and there are now at least three unrelated products using the Kuasa name — kuasa.ai, kuasa.io, and an open-source project called OpenKuasa which explicitly states in its README that it is unaffiliated with any company. When a category needs a disclaimer, the category is still being defined.

## What the agents are, as far as the evidence shows

ARA, HIRA and SAFA are presented as named employees. Naming is a good interface decision — a name is easier to talk about than a feature flag. It is not a capability.

The changelog for week three of November lists fixes and improvements across contacts, pipeline, forms, broadcasts and automations, plus Telegram receipt submission. That is the footprint of an assistant layered on a CRM: it reads what the CRM already stored and helps you act on it. Useful. Not an operating system, which would have to hold the ledger of the business rather than its conversations.

## What RM499 a month buys elsewhere

For the price of the top tier — RM5,988 a year — a Malaysian SME can buy a payroll service that calculates PCB correctly against the current schedule, or the accounting package that produces the SST-02 the return actually needs, or a year of both hosted tools and a freelancer to set them up.

That comparison is the whole argument. KuasaOS is not expensive in absolute terms; RM499 is a normal software bill. It is expensive **for a contact database**, and the pricing page gives no reason to think it is anything else.

## What would change our mind

Three things, all specific and all checkable:

1. A payroll run — EPF, SOCSO, EIS, PCB — calculated from the official tables, with the payslip produced.
2. An SST-02 summary, or an e-Invoice submission that goes through MyInvois and comes back with a validation link.
3. A pricing meter that charges per document or per run rather than per contact.

Ship any one of those and the phrase "business operating system" stops being decoration. Until then, the meter is the most honest sentence on the site.

## The claim

**A Malaysian business OS will be decided by PCB, SST-02 and e-Invoice, not by how well an agent is named — and at RM499 a month for a contact database, KuasaOS is selling the costume while the compliance work stays unbuilt. The first local product that files a return correctly will make every persona-named agent on the market look like what it is: a chat window with a business card.**

That is a wager, not a prophecy, and we will publish the correction the day a vendor proves it wrong.
