# e-Invoice exemption is RM3 million, not RM1 million

The exemption threshold is **RM3 million** of annual income or sales. LHDN's implementation timeline page, updated 30 August 2026 and read on 27 November 2026, says it in one line: taxpayers with annual income or sales of less than RM3,000,000 are exempt from e-Invoice implementation.

Most Malaysian articles still say RM1 million. That figure was published once and has not been corrected in the places it spread, which means a shop turning over RM2.4 million is currently being told by the internet that it has a compliance obligation it does not have.

## What the page states, and what it does not

The exemption clause is one sentence, and it is a revenue test. Annual income or sales, less than RM3 million, exempt. Nothing about headcount, nothing about registration type, nothing about how long you have been trading.

What the page does not state is more useful than most of what is written about it: whether the RM3 million is measured on a rolling twelve months or on a financial year; whether the band between RM3 million and RM5 million carries any grace period; whether particular industries are in scope regardless of turnover; and what happens to a business that crosses RM3 million partway through a year.

We are not going to guess at any of the four. Where the tax authority is silent, so are we.

## Headcount does not decide this

Two shops make the point faster than a definition:

- **Five staff, RM4 million turnover**, in scope. The payroll size is irrelevant; the revenue is over the line.
- **Twenty staff, RM2.5 million turnover**, exempt. Twice the people, half the revenue, no obligation.

That is why the exemption is routinely misapplied. Headcount is what an owner can see from their own payroll, so it is what they reason from. It also tells you about a different set of obligations entirely: the [EPF employer rate](https://upahje.my/en/articles/epf-employer-rate-malaysia), the [SOCSO employer rate](https://upahje.my/en/articles/socso-employer-rate-malaysia) and the [HRD Corp levy](https://upahje.my/en/articles/hrd-corp-levy-malaysia) all attach from hiring. e-Invoice attaches from turnover. Two calendars, two triggers, and conflating them produces wrong decisions in both directions, a small shop buying software it does not need, or a large one that never registered.

## The phase bands, and where the line now sits

For businesses above the exemption, implementation followed turnover bands, verbatim from the same dated page:

| Annual income or sales | Implementation date |
| --- | --- |
| More than RM100 million | 1 August 2024 |
| More than RM25 million and up to RM100 million | 1 January 2025 |
| More than RM5 million and up to RM25 million | 1 July 2025 |
| Up to RM5 million | 1 January 2026 |

Read the last two rows together and the practical position appears: a business at or above RM3 million but under RM5 million falls in the final band, and that date has already passed. The obligation is live now, not scheduled.

## Below the line, keep a record; above it, stop debating

**Our opinion, stated once:** the most valuable thing a sole operator can do this week is run the RM3 million test on their own books and write down which side of the line they are on, with the date they checked. Not on a colleague's recollection, not on an article published two years ago.

Below RM3 million, that note is your defence, and it should be re-tested as turnover approaches the threshold. At or above RM3 million, the decision is no longer about the threshold, it is about scope, and [what e-Invoice automation actually covers](https://upahje.my/en/articles/what-einvoice-automation-covers) is the next thing to read.
