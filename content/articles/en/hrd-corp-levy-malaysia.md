# HRD Corp levy: 1%, 0.5%, or nothing at all

A business with ten or more Malaysian employees pays 1% of monthly wages to HRD Corp. At five to nine employees the same levy is optional, at 0.5%. Below five there is no levy, a position the bands imply rather than print, and a first hire therefore carries nothing.

## The levy starts at five, and becomes compulsory at ten

The registered-employers page states that for employers with 10 or more Malaysian employees the levy is compulsory at 1% of monthly wages, and that employers with 5 to 9 are given the option to register at 0.5%.

A business with four employees is not named in either band. It sits below both, which is why a first hire carries no levy at all and why the total cost of that hire is the statutory trio and not a quartet. If the headcount is approaching five, treat the levy as starting before you hire, not after.

## What the levy is charged on

The percentage applies to the monthly wages of employees, so the ringgit figure is a share of payroll rather than a flat fee per head. The table below uses an average monthly wage of RM3,000 as a stated assumption, so a reader can substitute their own payroll and read the rate column, which is the sourced part.

| Malaysian employees | Rate | Monthly levy at RM3,000 average wage | Annual levy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | none | RM0 | RM0 |
| 5 | 0.5% | RM75 | RM900 |
| 9 | 0.5% | RM135 | RM1,620 |
| 10 | 1% | RM300 | RM3,600 |
| 25 | 1% | RM750 | RM9,000 |

The jump from nine to ten is the expensive one: the rate doubles and the headcount that crosses the line adds its own wage to the base in the same month.

## What changes at the boundary

A business at nine employees on a RM3,000 average wage pays RM135 a month. Hiring the tenth moves it to RM300, so the tenth salary costs the employer the wage, the statutory add-ons and an extra RM165 a month of levy.

Annualised, the step from nine to ten is RM1,980 in levy alone at that average wage, before the new employee's own EPF, SOCSO, EIS and any LINDUNG 24 Jam deduction.

## Registration runs on Form 1, and review is 30 days

A company with 10 or more Malaysian employees fills Form 1, and the page states that the application is reviewed by officers within 30 days. That review window is the only timing the page prints, so treat 30 days as a review period rather than a deadline you can plan a payroll around.

## No due date is printed

The registered-employers page does not state when the levy must be paid. That is an absence, not a gap to fill with a guessed date, so no payment deadline appears in this article. A business that needs the date should ask HRD Corp or read the levy rules rather than trust a template.

## What to do before the headcount moves

1. Count Malaysian employees only, because the bands are written that way.
2. Check whether you are at four, five or ten, since each is a different regime.
3. Register on Form 1 before assuming the 30-day review protects you.
4. Recalculate the month the tenth hire starts, not the month after.
5. Ask HRD Corp for the payment date in writing, because the page does not print one.

My opinion, stated once: the levy is small enough to ignore and large enough to notice at the ten-employee line, which is exactly the moment a business stops checking. Budgeting it at nine employees and discovering it at ten is a RM3,600-a-year surprise at a RM3,000 average wage, and that surprise lands in a month that is already busy with a new hire.
