# The licence stack a kedai pays, and two fees on record

The money in opening a kedai goes into the licence stack and the fit-out, not the website. Two councils publish itemised fees that can be read today — MBSA in Shah Alam and MBPJ in Petaling Jaya — and both are narrower than they look: one prints processing and sticker fees, the other prints signboard fees, and neither prints the annual premises licence itself.

## Two councils print two kinds of fee

MBSA prints the paperwork around a composite licence: RM50.00 to process a business application, RM100.00 for an industrial one, RM7.00 for the licence plate and RM5.00 for the sticker. Those are the fees for applying and for the physical things, not the annual licence charge.

MBPJ prints advertising fees. A non-illuminated signboard up to 8 m² is RM100.00 a year, with RM10.00 for each additional square metre or part of it; illuminated is RM150.00 a year with RM15.00 for each extra square metre; a logo-type advertisement is RM200.00; the guide also mentions a RM25.00 administration charge.

| Council | Item as the council prints it | Fee | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MBSA | Business application processing | RM50.00 | not the annual licence |
| MBSA | Industrial application processing | RM100.00 | not the annual licence |
| MBSA | Licence plate | RM7.00 | a physical item |
| MBSA | Licence sticker | RM5.00 | a physical item |
| MBPJ | Signboard, non-illuminated, up to 8 m² | RM100.00 a year | not the premises licence |
| MBPJ | Each extra m², non-illuminated | RM10.00 a year | applies above 8 m² |
| MBPJ | Signboard, illuminated, up to 8 m² | RM150.00 a year | not the premises licence |
| MBPJ | Each extra m², illuminated | RM15.00 a year | applies above 8 m² |
| MBPJ | Logo-type advertisement | RM200.00 | annual, per the same table |
| MBPJ | Administration charge | RM25.00 | mentioned in the guide |

Calling either of these the licence fee is the mistake this table exists to prevent. A reader who budgets RM50 for a Shah Alam licence has budgeted the processing fee.

## The stack has five parts, and four are still unpriced

A food premises licence stack in Malaysia is not one document. It is at least five:

1. **Business registration** with SSM, which depends on whether you are a sole proprietor or a company.
2. **Premises licence** from the council, which depends on the premises type, the floor area and the kitchen.
3. **Signboard or advertising licence**, which depends on lit or unlit, size, and whether the sign is a logo type.
4. **Food handler certification** for anyone touching food, which depends on the course provider.
5. **Halal certification**, only if you claim it, which is a separate application and a separate stack.

For four of those five, no fee could be read for this piece. DBKL's schedule sits behind its e-lesen portal, MBSJ's licensing page renders without a fee, and the food-handler course price was never read. Those cells stay empty here rather than being filled from memory.

## What to ask at the counter

1. Which licence is annual and which is a one-off processing fee?
2. What does the annual premises licence cost for this floor area and this kitchen?
3. Is the signboard fee per square metre, per year, or both?
4. Which certificate does the food handler need, and who issues it?
5. Which of these can be paid after opening, and which must be paid before?

Ask for the answers in writing, because the counter's answer is the only one that applies to your premises.

## What to do this month

1. List every licence your premises needs, using the five categories above.
2. Get the annual premises fee in writing from your council before signing a lease.
3. Price the signboard from the council's own schedule, not a contractor's estimate.
4. Put the two published council fees in the budget as line items, not as the whole stack.
5. Keep a folder for each licence, with the receipt and the renewal date.

An opening budget that treats the stack as one line is one surprise away from being wrong, and the surprise arrives at the counter.
