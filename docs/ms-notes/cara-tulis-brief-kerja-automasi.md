## Sentences written fresh rather than translated

1. `Brief yang menghalang anda daripada bayar dua kali` avoids the literal `brief yang menghalang anda daripada membayar dua kali` only by ellipsis; the Malay version drops the `mem-` prefix the way a Malaysian writer would.
2. `sebut harga yang tiba tanpa perkara-perkara ini bukan sebut harga` is a definitional sentence, not a translation. The English "is not a quote" carries emphasis the Malay repeats deliberately.
3. `Diam juga satu keputusan, dan keputusan yang mahal` restructures "silence is a decision too, and an expensive one".
4. `brief itu memberi ruang` for "the brief left room"; the literal `brief itu meninggalkan ruang` is the English word order.
5. `harga naik` rather than `harga meningkat` for "the price rises", chosen for the buyer's register.
6. `tiada satu pun daripada itu memerlukan freelancer yang tidak jujur` restructures the English sentence that starts with "None of that requires".

## For the native reader

- `serahan` for handover: correct, though `penyerahan` is also common. One spelling should be picked for the set; I used `serahan` in both places in this article.
- `ujian penerimaan` is the procurement term. If the audience is less formal, `ujian sebelum diterima` reads easier.
