## Sentences written fresh rather than translated

1. `Mengautomasikannya menimbus kekusutan di bawah tool dan bukan membetulkannya` keeps the image but uses BM verb order.
2. `Penyalinannya remeh` shortens "the typing is trivial" to two words, which BM does well.
3. `Hodoh, tetapi boleh dibaca` keeps the blunt aside rather than softening it.
4. `Selepas itu anda ada alasan berbentuk tool untuk tidak membetulkan punca asalnya` restructures "a tool-shaped reason not to fix it".
5. `Membosankan itulah maksudnya` keeps the spoken rhythm, without a particle.

## Register checks applied

- `anda` for the reader; `saya` where the piece admits its own limits, because an opinion piece is a person arguing.
- No particles, no `kalau`, no `macam mana`, no `yang mana`.
- `ialah` in definitional sentences only.
- No em dashes. No figures, no borrowed numbers.
- Malay written natively; the argument is the same, the sentences are not translations.

## For the native reader

- `salin-tampal` for copy-paste is natural Malaysian usage; `salin dan tampal` is longer and weaker in this rhythm.
- The heading `Di mana saya mungkin salah` carries the steelman. If house style prefers a question, `Di mana hujah ini mungkin salah?` works, at some cost in bluntness.
